Research

Interests and activities

My doctoral work mainly focuses on the analysis of hyperbolic partial differential equations, their numerical simulation and the identification of their parameters, with or without data.

Thesis topic

“Conservation laws with singular and nonlocal flux: numerical models and parameter identification”

This thesis project concerns the theoretical, numerical and inverse study of scalar conservation laws with singular and nonlocal flux, motivated by the modelling of goods transport devices (conveyor belts) and traffic dynamics. It aims to analyse hyperbolic models exhibiting an infinite speed of propagation, to develop robust numerical schemes (Finite Volumes, Wave-Front Tracking, Follow-the-Leader), and then to incorporate deep learning approaches for the identification of hidden parameters and data assimilation.

An open-source library will be built for the community, before addressing either the theoretical analysis of hybrid methods or the extension of the model to conveyor networks via Riemann solvers and a Hamilton–Jacobi reformulation.

Thesis prepared at the Institut Denis Poisson under the supervision of Boris Andreianov and Vincent Perrollaz. This description, taken from the thesis page on theses.fr, may be updated as the work progresses.
